**Steps** (read-only)
1. Log in on Suryodaya.
2. List all deals and all pipelines with the read-only list tools.
3. For each open deal (stage not closed won or closed lost), look up its stage in its pipeline.

**What we expected**
Every deal names the pipeline its stage belongs to, so the stage has a label, as on Keystone.

**What we see**
Some open Suryodaya deals have a stage key but no pipeline. Their stage can't be looked up, so it has no label.

**How the brief handles it** (agreed with the instructor, 24 Nov)
- These deals are **open**, judged by their stage key, and are ranked like any other deal.
- Each carries the data issue "pipeline: missing, so the stage has no label". Where the brief would show the stage label (for example, in a next step's reason), it shows the stage key instead.
- Nothing is guessed: the brief doesn't assign them to a pipeline.

**Where the code handles it**
- `brief/rows.py`: builds the row with no pipeline and no label, and adds the data issue. Its stage is marked as not known (`stage_known` is false), as it is for an unknown stage.
- `brief/deals.py` (Sunil, not written yet): must tell the two cases apart. A deal with **no pipeline** stays open and ranked. A deal whose stage is **not in its pipeline** is left out (task 3).
- Suite task 2 (`open_only`, Sunil, not written yet): checks that no-pipeline deals still count as open.
